#+TITLE: 16 · vterm (the compiled terminal)
#+PROPERTY: header-args:emacs-lisp :tangle yes

This module configures =vterm=, and nothing else. It used to also define
=my/toggle-terminal= and bind it to =C-`= and =C-c t t= — which
=essentials/17-terminal= defines and binds too, 34 lines of it, to the same two
keys.

Modules load in filename order, so 17 loaded second and won. Every line of the
toggle here was dead, and it had been dead since 17 was written: one want,
satisfied twice, the second written without deleting the first.

It could not have worked anyway. The dead toggle drove =eat=, and =eat= is not
in =emacs-package.nix= — on Nix, =use-package-always-ensure= is nil, so a
package that is not listed there is not installed and not on the load-path, the
=use-package= form is a silent no-op, and =eat-mode= is void. Two independent
reasons for the same twelve lines to do nothing, which is roughly how long
something survives when nothing ever calls it.

*The toggle belongs in 17 and stays there*, for a reason worth stating: this
module is *gated*. =init.el= only loads =16-vterm= where a =vterm-module= is
already on the load-path or cmake and a C compiler are present to build one.
=17-terminal= is ungated and always loads. A keybinding defined in a module that
is conditionally absent is a key that works on some machines — which is the
whole reason 17 exists.

So =C-`= opens 17's panel everywhere, and =M-x vterm= gets you the real terminal
on the machines that can build one.

* vterm
#+begin_src emacs-lisp
;;; 16-vterm.el --- Compiled terminal emulator (vterm) -*- lexical-binding: t; -*-

(use-package vterm
  :commands vterm
  :config (setq vterm-max-scrollback 10000))

(provide '16-vterm)
;;; 16-vterm.el ends here
#+end_src
